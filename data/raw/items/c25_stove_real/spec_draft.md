The SOTO WindMaster is a canister-mounted camping stove. With the four-arm pot support fitted it weighs 2.3 ounces, or 67 grams, a weight reviewers say disappears into a pack; the burner alone weighs 2.12 ounces. Stowed with the support fitted it measures 1.9 by 3.0 by 1.7 inches, and reviewers report that it packs bulkier than rival canister stoves because those support arms detach; the supports are fitted each use and go missing. Output is rated at 2800 kilocalories per hour, or 3260 watts and 11,000 British thermal units, which brings water up quickly. SOTO times two cups to a boil in wind in under two and a half minutes; independently measured boil times swing widely between test protocols, running from 2:27 to 4:02. One eight-ounce canister runs about ninety minutes. The burner takes butane, isobutane, and propane mixes, and pure propane canisters cannot be used. A concave burner head sits close under the pot and holds its flame in wind, a micro regulator valve holds output as the canister cools, and the flame turns down far enough to simmer. The four arms stay stable under a full pot; pots over two litres, or 1.5 litres of liquid, sit outside its limit. A piezo igniter is built into the burner post, and the die-cast aluminium body keeps it light and stiff. Included accessories: the four-arm pot support and a stuff sack. The three-arm support has to be bought separately. No fuel canister comes in the box, and no windscreen is supplied with the stove. Maintenance involves keeping the burner ports clear, checking the valve threads before each trip, and drying the supports before they are stowed again. The stove screws onto the canister thread by hand, and the arms swing out into place once it is seated.
