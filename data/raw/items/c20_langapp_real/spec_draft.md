Duolingo is a language-learning application offering over 280 courses covering more than 40 languages, with 148 of those courses added during 2025. The selection includes languages rarely taught elsewhere, and every course is reachable without payment on the free tier. Separate math, music, and chess tracks sit alongside the language courses. Lessons are short and built from reading, listening, and brief written or spoken responses rather than formal grammar instruction; grammar explanations are kept brief. A placement test at sign-up starts a returning learner partway into a course rather than at the beginning. Learners pick a daily goal, and streaks and experience points track it and sustain daily use. Weekly leaderboards group learners into leagues by points earned. The free tier shows advertisements between lessons and applies a hearts system that halts a session after a set number of mistakes. The paid tier removes the advertisements and the hearts limit, and adds downloading lessons for offline study; the free tier does not offer offline downloads. Clients run on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Apple TV, on Android devices, and in a web browser, with progress syncing across every signed-in device. Study within the application alone does not reach conversational fluency, and course depth varies considerably between languages. There is no live conversation practice with another person inside the application. Course progress produces no certificate; the maker's English test is a separate product. Pricing for the paid tier varies by region and by platform. Each unit closes with a review that revisits earlier material, and a learner resumes at the same point on any device after signing in. Exercises are delivered as translation, matching, and listening prompts, and a streak counter records the consecutive days on which the chosen daily goal was met.
