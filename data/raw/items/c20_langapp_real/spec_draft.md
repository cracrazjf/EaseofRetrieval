Duolingo is a language-learning application offering over 280 courses covering more than 40 languages, along with separate math, music, and chess tracks; 148 of those courses were added during 2025, and the selection includes languages rarely taught elsewhere. All courses are reachable without payment. Lessons are short and organised around streaks and experience points that sustain daily use. The free tier shows advertisements between lessons and applies a hearts system that halts a session after a set number of mistakes; the paid tier removes both. Clients run on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Apple TV, on Android devices, and in a web browser, with progress syncing across them. Course content is delivered as exercises in reading, listening, and short written or spoken responses rather than as formal grammar instruction, and grammar explanations are kept brief; each unit ends with a review that revisits earlier material. Independent reviews report that study within the app alone does not reach conversational fluency, and that course depth varies considerably between languages. Pricing for the paid tier varies by region and platform. A learner picks a daily goal at sign-up, and the streak counter tracks consecutive days on which that goal was met across every device.
