The Tessro Kettle is a gooseneck electric kettle for pour-over brewing. It measures 290 by 180 by 203 millimetres, weighs 1300 grams including its powered base, and holds 40 ounces, or 1.2 litres, which covers several cups from a single fill. It runs on 110- to 120-volt power at 1200 watts, 50 to 60 hertz. The body is 304 stainless steel with a BPA-free handle and lid pull, and the handle stays cool while the element runs. The lid lifts off completely for filling and for reaching inside to clean. Five preset buttons select temperatures of 140, 160, 175, 195, and 212 degrees Fahrenheit, shown on a backlit segment display that stays readable in a dim kitchen; temperature cannot be set to a specific degree between those five presets, and there is no altitude adjustment for the boiling point. A hold mode keeps the selected temperature for thirty minutes and cannot be extended past that. The kettle has no scheduling function, so heating cannot be started at a set time, and it has no brew stopwatch to time the pour. A boil-dry cutoff shuts the element off when the kettle runs empty. The gooseneck spout is shaped for directed pouring and pours deliberately slowly. The cord measures three feet, so the base reaches an outlet set back from the counter, and the powered base occupies its own area of counter. At 203 millimetres tall the kettle sits under a wall cabinet. Included accessories: the kettle body and its powered base. The kettle lifts off the base in any orientation, and the base holds the cord underneath. Maintenance involves periodic descaling of the stainless interior, since scale builds up with use, wiping the exterior after use, and keeping the base contacts free of mineral residue. The display, the five buttons, and the on-off switch are the whole of the interface.
