The Kelvane Slate 65 is a wired mechanical keyboard with 68 keys in a 65 percent ANSI layout. The F-keys, home, end, page up and page down are all reached through a layer combination rather than a dedicated function row or a separate navigation cluster. The aluminium frame measures 315 by 105 millimetres and weighs about 810 grams, a mass that keeps the board planted on a desk but makes it heavy to carry anywhere. Case height with keycaps fitted is 32 millimetres at the front and 42 at the rear, and no wrist rest is supplied. The plate is gasket mounted over foam damping, which softens the sound of each press. Hot-swappable sockets accept linear or tactile switches without soldering; switches are bought separately from the board. Dye-sublimated PBT keycaps carry legends that do not wear off with use. The backlight emits white light only, in eight patterns across four brightness levels. USB Type-C is the board's only connection, with no Bluetooth or wireless receiver; the cable detaches at the board for storage and tethers the board to the machine while in use. Polling runs at 1000 hertz with full N-key rollover across every key. The 68-key layout keeps a dedicated arrow cluster and a delete key in the right-hand column, so the arrow keys stay off the layers. Foam under the plate damps the case as well, so the board sounds lower than an unfilled one. Both macOS and Windows layouts are supported through a switch on the underside, which changes the modifier order in hardware. There is no configuration software, so that switch is the only way to change what the keys send. Included accessories: a detachable USB-C cable, a keycap puller, and a switch puller. Maintenance involves lifting the keycaps periodically to clear dust from the plate, and the same board moves between machines and works as soon as it is plugged in.
