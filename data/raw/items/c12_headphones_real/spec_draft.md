The Sony WH-1000XM4 is a wireless over-ear headphone with active noise canceling. The headset weighs 254 grams and uses 40-millimetre drivers with liquid crystal polymer diaphragms; reviewers describe the consumer tuning as satisfying across genres. Active noise canceling works from dual sensors in each cup and is rated among the strongest in its class. Padded earcups and a padded headband stay comfortable across long sessions. Connection is Bluetooth 5.0, with LDAC at up to 990 kilobits per second and multipoint pairing to two devices at once; multipoint falls back to AAC or SBC, so LDAC is unavailable while two devices are connected. There is no aptX or aptX HD support, and no IP water resistance rating of any kind. Frequency response is 20 hertz to 20 kilohertz on standard codecs, extending to 40 kilohertz over LDAC at a 96-kilohertz sampling rate. Sony rates battery life at up to 30 hours with noise canceling on, a figure that holds only with AAC or SBC and with DSEE Extreme switched off; with LDAC or DSEE Extreme active, playback falls to between 16 and 18 hours. With noise canceling off the rating is 38 hours, talk time is 24 hours, and wired playback runs 36 hours. A 10-minute charge returns about five hours of playback. DSEE Extreme upscales compressed files while they play, and it is the same setting that shortens the rated playing time. A touch gesture panel on the right cup handles playback and calls, and reviewers report it registering taps inconsistently. Speak-to-chat pauses playback when it hears the wearer, and reviewers report it triggering on coughs and laughter as well. Included accessories: a USB-C cable, a 3.5-millimetre cable, a hard carrying case, and an in-flight plug adaptor. Maintenance involves wiping the earpads and keeping the charging port clear of lint.
