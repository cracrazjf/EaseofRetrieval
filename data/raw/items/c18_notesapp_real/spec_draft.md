Notion is a workspace application in which pages and databases are assembled from blocks that can be nested, linked, and reused, so one workspace replaces several separate tools; setup and the advanced features take weeks to learn. Content is organised into shared workspaces with per-page permissions and guest access. Four plans are offered: free, Plus, Business, and Enterprise. The free plan gives individuals unlimited blocks and is usable for individual work, with limits applied to multi-member workspaces, a 5-megabyte cap on file uploads, ten guests, and seven days of page history. AI features are trial-limited on the free and Plus plans and included on Business and Enterprise. Pages export as PDF, HTML, or Markdown, and databases export straight out to CSV. Pages marked available offline can be read on the desktop and mobile clients, though offline mode blocks new pages, uploads, AI, and formula recalculation. Performance degrades on databases past several thousand rows, and the mobile client lags behind the desktop app. A large template library and community guides support new users. Clients exist for the web, macOS, Windows, iOS, and Android. Blocks moved between pages keep their formatting and their links, and databases can be shown as tables, boards, calendars, or galleries.
