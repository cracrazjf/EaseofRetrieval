Notion is a workspace application in which pages and databases are built from blocks that can be nested, linked, and reused. One workspace of pages and databases replaces several separate tools, though setup and the advanced features take weeks to learn. Databases display as tables, boards, calendars, timelines, or galleries, and the same records appear in every view. Content sits in shared workspaces with per-page permissions and guest access. Search runs across every page and database in a workspace. Four plans are offered: free, Plus, Business, and Enterprise. The free plan gives an individual unlimited blocks and is usable on its own for individual work; once a workspace has more than one member the block allowance is capped, so blocks are not unlimited for teams. The free plan also limits file uploads to 5 megabytes, caps guests at ten, and keeps seven days of page history. AI features are not included beyond a trial on the free and Plus plans, and are included in full on Business and Enterprise. Pages export as PDF, HTML, or Markdown, and databases export as CSV. Pages marked available offline can be read on the desktop and mobile clients without a connection, but offline mode does not allow new pages, uploads, AI, or formula recalculation. Performance degrades on databases past several thousand rows. The mobile client lags behind the desktop app. A large template library and community guides cover common setups. Clients exist for the web, for macOS and Windows on the desktop, and for iOS and Android on mobile. Blocks moved between pages keep their formatting and their links, and a database in one page can be referenced from another without copying the underlying records. Any page can be published to a public web address, and every change is attributed to the member who made it.
