YNAB is a budgeting application built around zero-based budgeting, in which every available dollar is assigned to a spending or saving category before it is spent. The zero-based method takes weeks to learn, and categorising and reconciling transactions by hand stays regular work once the budget is running. Clients run on the web, on iOS, and on Android, and changes sync automatically across devices; there is no installed desktop application, so desktop use happens in a browser. Linked bank accounts import transactions automatically, and the bank import and reconciliation work reliably. Apple Card activity imports alongside ordinary bank accounts. Category targets track progress toward planned amounts, and a loan planner projects interest and payoff timing. The category targets and the debt payoff tools cover planned spending and debt repayment. Spending charts and net worth charts report activity over time. There is no bill tracking and no bill payment, and there is no credit score monitoring. One subscription covers up to six people, so a household shares a single plan, and each member holds their own login to the same budget. New users get a 34-day trial without entering a card, and students get a 365-day trial. Plan data and transaction history export as CSV or TSV files from the web app only; the mobile clients do not export. All features sit on one paid tier, and there is no free tier for continued use once a trial ends. The subscription is among the more expensive budgeting subscriptions, with a price that has risen repeatedly. Accounts are grouped into budget and tracking accounts, and every transaction is matched to a category before the month is closed; overspending in one category is covered by moving money across from another.
