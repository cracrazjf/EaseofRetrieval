YNAB is a budgeting application built around zero-based budgeting, in which every available dollar is assigned to a spending or saving category before it is used; the method takes weeks to learn. The service runs on the web, iOS, and Android, syncing automatically across devices. Linked bank accounts import transactions, including Apple Card activity, and users reconcile them against assigned categories, which reviewers report works reliably though reconciliation still takes regular hand work. Category targets track progress toward planned amounts, and a loan planner projects interest and payoff timing. Spending and net worth reports chart activity over time. There is no bill tracking and no bill payment. A single subscription covers up to six people, so households share one plan. New users get a 34-day trial without entering a card, and students get a 365-day trial. Plan data and transaction history export from the web app only, as CSV or TSV files. All features sit on a single paid tier, among the more expensive budgeting subscriptions, whose price has risen repeatedly; there is no free tier for ongoing use after the trial ends.
