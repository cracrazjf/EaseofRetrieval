The Vestrino M2 is a pump-driven espresso machine for home use. It measures 145 by 320 by 310 millimetres and weighs 4.0 kilograms. A 15-bar pump feeds a thermoblock heating system. Because a single thermoblock serves both functions, brewing and steaming cannot run at the same time and a cool-down wait separates them. The removable water tank holds 1.0 litres, lifts out from the top of the housing, and carries a level window on the side. Two illuminated buttons control single and double espresso, with a separate dial for steam; shot volume is programmable across two levels, and brewing temperature is fixed. A manual frother switches between steamed milk and froth and also dispenses hot water for tea, and reaching fine microfoam depends on technique. The 53-millimetre filter holder takes ground coffee for one or two cups using two pressurised baskets, a size with a narrower aftermarket than the common 51-millimetre fitting; it does not accept ESE pods, and the machine has no grinder. A removable two-level drip tray takes cups up to 11 centimetres tall. There is no cup rest on top of the housing and no pressure gauge on the panel, so shots are judged by eye and by timing. The machine drops into standby 15 minutes after the last brew and draws 1250 watts while heating. A descaling light on the panel signals when treatment is due, and water hardness is set with a dip strip supplied in the box rather than through a menu. Included accessories: a combined measuring scoop and tamper moulded as one plastic piece, and a water hardness test strip. The portafilter seats with a quarter turn, and the group head clears the drip tray by enough to swap baskets without lifting the machine. Maintenance involves rinsing the tank and tray.
