The Dovrell 230 is a rechargeable electric toothbrush. The head vibrates at sonic frequency against the tooth surface rather than rotating, a motion that feels different to anyone used to oscillating rotation, and one that runs quieter than an oscillating handle. The motor ramps up gradually at the start of a session rather than reaching full speed at once. A pressure sensor eases output when the user presses too hard against the gums. A timer runs for two minutes and pauses every thirty seconds to pace movement between quadrants of the mouth. A light on the handle shows the charge remaining. The handle carries a rubberised grip that holds steady in a wet hand, and it accepts three head shapes from one maker and no others, so heads come from one maker and nowhere else. Charging is over a USB-C port in the base of the handle, so the cable travels wherever the brush goes and the charging port sits exposed in a wet bathroom; there is no charging stand to hold the brush upright on a bathroom counter. A full charge lasts about three weeks of twice-daily use. Included accessories: one brush head, a hard travel case that bulks up a packed washbag, and a USB-C cable. The product carries a two year warranty. Maintenance involves rinsing the head and handle under a running tap after each use, standing both to dry, and replacing the head once the bristles begin to splay outward from the centre of the brush face. A full charge from empty takes about four hours over the USB-C cable. The travel case holds the handle, one head, and the cable together, and closes with a clasp. The brush head clips onto a shaft at the top of the handle and pulls off by hand.
