The De'Longhi Dedica EC685 is a pump-driven espresso machine for home use. It measures 149 by 330 by 305 millimetres, narrower than most machines in its class, and weighs 4.22 kilograms. A 15-bar pump feeds a thermoblock heating system, and warm-up takes 30 to 40 seconds. The single thermoblock serves both brewing and steaming, so they cannot run at once and a cool-down wait separates them. The removable water tank holds 1.1 litres, slides out from the rear, and carries a level window on the side. Three illuminated buttons control single espresso, double espresso, and steam, and both shot volume and brewing temperature are programmable, the latter across three levels. The panel carries no pressure gauge. Water hardness is set through the same menu, and a descaling light on the panel signals when treatment is due. A manual Panarello frothing sleeve switches between steamed milk and froth and also dispenses hot water for tea; milk is textured by hand, with no automatic milk system fitted, and fine microfoam takes practice with this stock wand. The 51-millimetre filter holder takes ground coffee for one or two cups as well as ESE paper pods, using pressurised baskets that put a ceiling on espresso clarity; a wide aftermarket of 51-millimetre baskets and bottomless portafilters fits it. The machine does not grind beans, so coffee must be ground first. A removable two-level drip tray takes cups up to 12 centimetres tall, and tall cups fit only once the tray is removed. A cup rest on top warms cups. The machine shuts off 20 minutes after the last brew, standby is adjustable, and it draws 1300 watts while heating. Brew temperature runs near 65 degrees Celsius unless holder and cup are preheated, and the light body shifts as the portafilter is seated. Included accessories: a combined measuring scoop and tamper; no milk pitcher is supplied. Maintenance involves rinsing the tank and drip tray.
